Second pass over the DNA files the owner gave this agent on 14 November 2026, made after he asked why this agent had only counted. Read by program; no file and no tester's given name is in this repository. Everything here is scored as given. The meaning of amounts of shared DNA is from recall of the usual tables, not checked.

## 2. His parents were not cousins

Štefan's own genome has no long stretch where the copy from his father and the copy from his mother are the same: none of 4 million letters or more, and only two over 1.5 million. The owner's file, run the same way as a check, gives the same kind of answer. A child of first or second cousins would show several long stretches. So Michal Duska of Senné and Maria Demko of Iňačovce were not closely related when they married in 1927. Third cousins or further is not excluded. The method is a plain count on chip data, not a validated tool (`DM-H-048`).

This matters for both lines: no shared great-grandparent is to be looked for, and a match of Štefan's belongs to one side or the other, not both.

## 3. Old surnames of Viszoka among the small matches

Three matches carry Tirpák, as their own name or a forebear's: 23, 15 and 13 cM. A Mária Demko of Viszoka married György Tirpák about 1830 and died there in 1857 (`DM-D-119`). Her descendants would be fifth cousins or so to Štefan, which is what such amounts look like. One match lists Hreško among forebears (14 cM), one Cservenyák (16 cM), the name of a Kocsis wife at Solymos in the 1840s. Each alone is noise. Together they are the right names from the right two villages, and nothing more than that.

No Kocsis and no Knyezső in any spelling. The mother's mother's side has left nothing in this list.

## 5. Where his distant matches come from

Of 1,945 matches, 620 list forebears. Most of the list is small matches with English names, which is the company's customers and chance. Among matches of 12 cM and more, the forebear surnames that recur and are not English are Hudák (the largest such match is 34 cM), Barna, Bodnár, Drozd, Kozak, Kowal, Baran, Kulyk, Sikora, Papp, Petryshyn, Wesołowski, Grabowski. Four rows use the word Rusyn or Ruthenian; none says Slovak. Read plainly, his detectable kin are from eastern Slovakia and the Polish and Ukrainian side of the Carpathians.

The limit is large: American descendants of Carpathian emigrants test far more than people in Slovakia do, and Greek Catholic emigrants of the 1890s are a big share of them. So the list shows who tested, as much as who he was. It does not separate his father's side from his mother's.
