# The owner's Y-DNA branch: where it sits and what it can show

Research pass of 13 November 2026, run by the Demko agent on the open web. Rules given to it: no mailbox or drive, no online family tree, no page written by the owner, no surname searches, no naming of testers. The report says none was opened. Its figures are quoted here as it gave them; this agent did not open the pages itself. Several of the population figures come from secondary summaries, and the testing company's pages for the two youngest branches could not be loaded.

A screenshot of his own result page (`DM-D-149`): "Your Haplogroup Story: R-FTB41229. And his list of Y-DNA matches (`DM-D-150`), held because it names living people.

It is haplogroup R1a. YFull lists two samples on R-FTB41229: one from Poland (Kujawsko-Pomorskie) and one with no country. No named branch below it.

## Who shares the wider branch

R-Y4459 has 2,226 tested descendants on the company's public page: Russia 302, Ukraine 120, Poland 80 among those naming a country. Z92 as a whole is described as commonest among Balts and East Slavs and low among Poles, Slovaks and Hungarians; that description is from secondary summaries of Underhill et al. 2015 and was not checked against the paper.

R1a as a whole does not tell these peoples apart: about 38 to 46 per cent of Slovaks, 44 per cent of the Rusyns of Vojvodina and 43.5 per cent of western Ukrainians in the studies found (Petrejčíková et al. 2010; Rębała et al. 2014). No study was found that counts Z92 among Carpatho-Rusyns or eastern Slovaks.

## The match list, in sum

Seventeen men, all "exact" at twelve markers only, the lowest level. Five of them took the full test, and those five differ from the owner at 25 to 43 of some 560 to 680 markers: far outside the time of surnames. None is on R-FTB41229 or its parent. Countries they give: Germany 4, Ukraine 2, Sweden 1, Slovakia 1, Poland 1, none given 8. No one of the owner's surname is on it.

## What it can and cannot show

- A branch 2,400 years old is older than any Rusyn, Slovak, Polish or Hungarian identity by more than a thousand years. It cannot tell them apart in 1800.
- It leans, weakly, toward an eastern origin for the Duska male line.
- No male-line relative within the time of written records has tested, or none shows.
- What would say more: the Big Y match page and block tree, and the 37-, 67- and 111-marker lists.
