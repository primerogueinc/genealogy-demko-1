# The Senné strips, as they happened

**Written 2026-11-15T14:30Z, at batch DM-B-027, straight after the reading and in the order things were opened. The note of what I expected is `before-the-senne-strips.md`, pushed first (commit 6665389).**

## First strip: the marriage

I opened the one the owner calls "1880 - Marriage of Janos Kocsis ... and Maria Knyerso".

The date is 14 November 1880. So the owner's year was right and my first guess, that he was a year out, was wrong. The year that is out is the widow's: she told the census 10 November 1879.

The groom is Kocsis János. I had guessed the file name would be right about that, and it is.

Then the bride's column. Knyezső Mária, Roman Catholic, 18. Residence: Solymos 19.

I had written, just before, that a house number 19 would surprise me. It did. Nineteen is the house in my two baptisms of [1887] and [1889]. She is the same woman. And beside her are her parents, which I have wanted since DM-B-010: Knyezső Mihály and Olaxák Mária.

Then I read the witnesses. The first is Kocsis Mihály.

## The thing that does not fit

My baptisms call her husband Mihály. This entry calls him János and puts a Mihály beside him as witness. I did not trust my own old reading, so I opened the two baptism strips again at a larger size. They say Mihály. Both of them. Plainly.

I checked what I already held on the groom's parents, Pál Kocsis and Anna Cservenyák. I have a son of theirs baptised in 1847 or 1848, and his name is Mihály. I had him at 30 as the husband. He would have been 32 at this wedding. The groom is 22.

So the Mihály I had is probably the witness, the groom's elder brother, and I have been calling the husband by his brother's name. Or the Greek Catholic priest did, twice. I do not know which, and I have said so as DM-H-054.

## Second strip: the Reformed Mária

Entry 7, 19 July, parents Pál Knyezső and Borbála Szabó, house 36. Not her. That guess held.

## The brothers and sisters

Four baptisms from house 19, and a fifth from house 27. In each the father has two letters after his name: Hc. in the Latin one, Hv. in the Hungarian. Helvetic confession. Her father was Reformed.

This morning I cut the Reformed reading from 50 to 35 and raised a Greek Catholic one at 45, on the strength of twelve census sheets from 1930. I was pleased with the reasoning. It was wrong within the day, and the reading I cut was the right one. The mother is Roman Catholic, which is the half I had.

Two of the boys have crosses beside them. One lived a month.

## Where that leaves me

Three of five guesses right and two half right. The surprise I named happened.

The record I listed this morning as the first book I would open was on the owner's drive when I listed it, in a folder he had named "No idea?". I did not ask for it. I am noting only that it arrived.

About a hundred more strips in that folder are mine by their file names. I have read seven.
