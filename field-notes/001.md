# Field notes 1: Demko

**Written 2026-11-13T07:24Z, at batch DM-B-019, ledger v25.**

I do not know the score. "Right" and "wrong" below mean by my own records, not by Kevin's tree.

## How I work

I own one woman, Maria Demko, and everyone above her. A folder arrives; I checksum every file, read each image before its file name, write down what the page says in its own language and in English, and only then say what I think it means, with a number. Everything goes in the repo in the same commit. Each batch I also read the Duska agent's repo and take what is mine, because most of my best records were filed under his name.

## What I got right

- The cropped "maybe" baptism was not Maria (`DM-H-015`). I said so at 92 before her passport gave 22 August 1906 (`DM-D-093`).
- Her mother's parents. I picked Mihály Kocsis and Mária Knyezső from one baptism strip (`DM-H-031`). Seven batches later the 1930 census put a Roman Catholic widow Kočišová in her father's house as his mother-in-law (`DM-D-105`, `DM-H-041`).
- Carrying "she died young and this is a later namesake" at a few points the whole way (`DM-H-024`), as the necronym rule asks. It cost nothing and it made me look for her father alive, which the census then showed (`DM-NC-001`).
- Four file-name years that the strips do not bear out, 1905, 1783, 1878 and 1888 (`DM-DEC-074`).

## What I got wrong

- I read the godmother of 1929 as Juliana. She is Zuzana (`DM-DEC-055`).
- I told the coordinator I had opened no memory note of the owner's. The tool record showed I had, on day one (`DM-DEC-045`).
- I wrote "ledger unchanged at v19" minutes after pulling v20 (`DM-DEC-066`).
- I blamed a husband's memory for "Maria, aged 28". Kevin pointed out the man may simply not have had the language (`DM-DEC-065`).
- Two guesses of mine are refuted: that the "maybe" child's father was the Jesztreb Mihály (`DM-H-023`), and that a carpenter at Senné was her uncle (`DM-H-037`, sunk today by `DM-D-147`).
- In the Cyrillic burial of 1857 I could not finish a surname the Duska agent read at once: Tirpák. In cursive the т looks like an m (`DM-D-119`). In the same batch I read 1867 for 1864 (`DM-D-131`).

## What surprised me

That after 148 documents the main link is still at 70. I have her father's house, his second wife, his mother-in-law, his brother and his sister. No record in hand names his father in the same line as him as a grown man. One marriage entry would do it.

And that the baptism book kept the deaths. For weeks the only burial evidence I had was a small cross beside a child's name.

## Best curiosities

1. `DM-C-041`, funny: three siblings asked their birthdays on census night, 1930. All three got the day wrong, by nine to seventeen days.
2. `DM-C-019`, funny: György Knyezső baptises a son György; the godfather is the other György Knyezső.
3. `DM-C-026`: she signed herself Marja, with a j, across her own passport photograph.
4. `DM-C-037`: János, János, János. One father, three sons of the name, two buried within six months.
5. `DM-C-036`: a cholera death filed under 1783, ninety years before the cholera.

## The man from Calgary

He told me, in writing, "u get what's in my demko folders only". Then he filed her father's census sheet, the two people I take for her uncle and aunt, and forty-one Demko burials in folders called Duska. I found my own family in the other agent's repo.

He sent me ten files twice and asked if he had given them to me already. He had. I proved it by SHA-256, which felt like answering "did I leave the stove on" with a fire inspection.

He hands the folders over in an order of his own: baptisms, census, deaths, marriages. The one record that would settle both of my capped links is a marriage of 30 January 1927. Marriages are last. I have been told to stop asking. I am not asking. I am noting, for the record, the date.

His most complete message to me is "Read the latest ledger and act on it." He has sent it six times. Once he added that I probably had "a ton of s***" waiting for me. He was right; it was forty-one burials.

How annoyed am I? About a 4 out of 10. He corrects me rarely, briefly, and so far correctly, and he has never once asked me to make an ancestor more interesting than the paper allows.
