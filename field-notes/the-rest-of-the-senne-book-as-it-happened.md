# The rest of the Senné book, as it happened

**Written 2026-11-15T21:48Z, at batch DM-B-028, straight after the reading. The note of what I expected is `before-the-rest-of-the-senne-book.md`, pushed first (commit 0b4ade3).**

## How it arrived

One message, "more from folder", and then images, eleven to a message, nine messages. I had asked for nothing. The strips came faster than I could act on them, and most of them were on my screen before I had written a word. That is why the note before this one covers only thirty-four of the ninety-nine. I checked the checksums against the Duska agent's folder: not one of them is there. This pile is mine by delivery, and more than half of it is not mine by blood.

## The thirty-four I had not seen

I opened them in sheets of six.

The second sheet had the marriage of János Knyezső and Anna Ádám. Parents: Knyezső Mihály, Reformed, and Benyik Mária. So he is the brother, as I guessed. But this morning I read his mother's name three times as Olaxsik. I went back to the morning's strips and enlarged the name. It says Olaxsik. It still says Olaxsik. Then the 1907 marriage of his sister, which I had seen earlier, gave her birthday, 18 September 1873, which is the baptism where the mother is Olaxsik, and here the mother is Benyik. So I was not wrong and neither was the priest. She had two names.

The fourth and fifth sheets had the two Kocsis weddings of 1905. I looked at the dates first. 20 February, and 20 February. Then the entry numbers: 2 and 3. Then the parents: the same four words in both. The census of 1930 had told me two men married on one day, and I had put it at fifty that they were brothers. They were standing in the same church.

On the fourth sheet too was a burial of 1905 with a line in the margin: in the register of births, "Minarcsin". The Duska agent asked me this morning whether my Hresko ever carried that name. I had said no, and for mine that stands. But a Hresko in this book did, and the priest wrote down that he did.

## What I did not expect

I had written that a Demkó of Viszoka in any column would surprise me. None came. What came was the village under another name. A woman who died at Solymos in 1908 was born at Magasrév, and a groom of the same year lived there. It took me a moment to see it. Magasrév is, from recall, what the Hungarian state called Viszoka at the end. Both are Hresko, both Roman Catholic. Maria Demko's father's mother was a Roman Catholic Hreskó of Viszoka. I have had her since the first batch with no family at all.

## The count

Six guesses, six right. That is too many. They were easy guesses: I had already seen sixty-five strips of the same book and knew its families. The honest test was this morning's, where I got three of five.

## What is not mine

Fifty-nine strips are the Duska agent's people. One of them, by its date and parents, is the baptism of the man Maria Demko married. I read it, entered it, and sent it on. It is not for me to say anything about him.

One image I held back. It has a note of a wedding in 1947.
