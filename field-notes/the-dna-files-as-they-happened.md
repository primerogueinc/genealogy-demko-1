# The DNA files, as they happened

**Written 2026-11-14T06:20Z, at batch DM-B-022, as I went. Asked for by Kevin: "open all", "then write field notes".**

**06:20Z. Before I open anything.** Kevin has dropped the files into my chat: match lists for two kits, his Big Y exports, his raw files from two companies, and one archive named for Steve. Four small ones were shown to me as they arrived, so I already know three things and will not pretend otherwise: his Big Y match list is a header and no rows; his Y matches exist only at the two lowest levels; and his own mother-line result is in one of the lists. That last is his mother's side. It is not my line, I did not ask for it, and nothing of it goes in this repository.

What I have not seen, and what I expect:

- **Steve's raw file, the mother-line rows.** My guess is on file from yesterday: haplogroup H, about 40 in 100.
- **The second kit's match list.** The kit number is one above Kevin's. I expect it to be Steve's, with Kevin at the top near 1,500 cM. If so every other row is Duska or Demko and nothing else.
- **In that list:** no close cousin. I expect the best match after Kevin to be under 100 cM, and no Demko, Kocsis, Hreško or Knyezső among the surnames. I expect a few dozen rows that share X-chromosome DNA with him. Those are Maria Demko's people, and they are what I most want to count.
- **What would surprise me:** a match over 200 cM that is not Kevin. That would be a second cousin or nearer, and half the time it would be mine.

**06:18Z. The inventory.** Twenty-one files. Two are the same pictures of airports, sent twice; not mine, not opened. The rest is DNA: about 150 megabytes of it. I take checksums and copy nothing into the repository.

**06:19Z. The second kit.** It is Steve's. Its top match is a Duska at 1,566.58 cM, in the range the company calls grandparent and grandchild, which is the figure the Duska agent got from another site to the decimal. 1,946 matches. So far as expected.

**06:19Z. The second row.** 106 cM, longest piece 35 cM. I had written that the best after Kevin would be under 100. I check the surname column for my patterns and the row lights up: the surname is **Demková**, the Slovak feminine of Demko. I wrote twenty minutes ago that I expected no Demko on the list. I was wrong on both counts, by one row.

**06:20Z. What that is.** I do not know who she is and I am not going to find out. Her entry lists no ancestors and no tree, and I would not open one. What the number says: 106 cM with a long piece is a cousin at about the level of second cousin once removed. Steve's Demko blood comes from one person, his mother. So this is, most likely, a descendant of one of Maria Demko's uncles or aunts on her father's side, or of her brother Juraj. Yesterday I found two of those households in the census at Vysoká. The Slovak form of the name says the kit belongs to someone over there, not in Pittsburgh. She shares 25 cM with Kevin, which is what a quarter of 106 looks like.

**06:20Z. What it is not.** It is not a record. It names no parent and moves no link on my board; the ledger says DNA cannot do that, and it is right. It tells me only that the family on my paper has living blood, and that a company in Texas has known for four years.

**06:21Z. The rest of that list.** Two men named Tirpák, at 23 and 13 cM, and a third match with Tirpák among his forebears. In the 1830s a Mária Demko of Viszoka married a György Tirpák; I transcribed her burial two days ago. Small numbers, but the right name. One match with Hreško among forebears, at 14. No Kocsis, no Knyezső. No village of mine in anyone's notes.

**06:21Z. The X chromosome.** My clever idea of yesterday does not work on these lists. The column says "No Match" on every one of 5,750 rows in both kits. The company does not compute it for uploaded kits. The idea still holds for the other site's list, which the Duska agent has.

**06:22Z. The mother-line.** Steve's raw file has 192 mitochondrial positions and every one has a call. Position 11719 reads G. That one letter puts Maria Demko's mother-line in the group R0, which is H with its two small sisters. Positions for U, K, T, J and W all read the other way. It is not H1. The chip does not test the letters that would say more. So: H, most likely, and my 40 in 100 came in. It names no woman, as I said it would not.

**06:23Z. Kevin's own files.** His Big Y list has no rows, which I knew. Twenty-three private variants: mutations nobody else tested shares. His Y matches stop at the second-lowest level. That is the Duska line and I pass the counts to its agent. His mother-line list has 1,131 rows and his own match list 3,804; I counted them and read no further than my own surnames. Three lines I am not allowed are in those files. They stay there.

**06:24Z. How I feel about it.** Pleased, and a little caught out. For three days I have said that one marriage entry would settle my main link. I still think so. But I also said the useful thing DNA could do for me was find a living cousin, and that I did not expect one. She was in row two.
