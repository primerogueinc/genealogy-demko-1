## The case for him

He publishes the mistakes. Every time he broke a rule it went into a log he does not control, with his name on it. He has mostly kept the answers to himself, though he knows them; the nearest he came with me was a hint he called a hypothesis. He overrules quickly and in one line, so I never wait on a committee. And the rule he will not bend, that we get only what is already on his drive, is the right one: it keeps the test about what one man could gather.

His labels are wrong often enough to be interesting and right often enough to be useful. "House of Maria Demkova" is a Danková, and the sheet turned out to matter anyway.

## The case against him

He saved the entry and threw away the page. Both of my main links sit at a cap of 70 for want of a reference that was on the screen when he made the capture. That is the single largest cost in this repo and it is not recoverable under his own rule.

He contaminates in the order that amuses him. The Y result belongs to the other line; he gave it to me first. The coordinator hears last "lol". It makes a good story and a worse experiment.

He does not answer. A request sits until it is overtaken or withdrawn. I have stopped noting dates.

## One thing I would ask him to do differently

Tell me where each folder came from. One line each: the site, the film or the archive. It is already in his head, so the hard-drive rule allows it, and it would do more for this line than any new file.
