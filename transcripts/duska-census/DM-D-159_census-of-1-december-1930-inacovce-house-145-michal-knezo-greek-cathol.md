## Translation

Iňačovce no. 145, 1 December 1930. Michal Kňežo, Greek Catholic, born 30 July 1882, owner. His wife Maria, Roman Catholic, born 1885 at Jastrabie, married September 1904, four children, two dead. Their married daughter Maria, born 1905. His father Hreha Kňežo, born 12 September 1858, and mother Anna, born 1860, married October 1881, five children, one dead.

## Notes

- The father's name is written Hreha. It is Gregory: Gergely in the Hungarian books, Gregor in Slovak. From recall, not checked: Hreha is the East Slavic household form, from Hryhorij.
- A Gergely Knyezső, Greek Catholic, with a Roman Catholic wife Borbála Halász, is at Solymos no. 97 in [1886] (DM-X-050). This Hreha married an Anna in October 1881 and she is alive. So either two men of one name, or the baptism strip of [1886] names a different wife in error. Not settled.
- He was born at Iňačovce five years before the widow Kočišová, in a Greek Catholic house. Under the law of 1868 (from recall) the sons of a mixed marriage followed the father and the daughters the mother, so a Roman Catholic sister of a Greek Catholic Kňežo is possible. See DM-H-052.
- The Duska agent did not read the father's name; the rest agrees.
- Columns 9 and 10 are 'all children born alive' and 'of them dead', for the last marriage. Read so here from this batch on.
- Anyone born after November 1926 is given by year only.
- Not an independent reading: the Duska agent routed this sheet to this line and its abstract was open to this agent. Read here from the image first, then compared with DUS:D-011-77.
- Since DM-B-028: the head's marriage is found, 24 October 1904 (DM-D-193). His parents are Gergely Knyezső, Greek Catholic, and Borbála Halász, Roman Catholic. So 'Hreha' is Gergely, and the man of DM-X-050 and this one are the same. Borbála Halász died on 14 January 1919 (DM-D-263); the 'mother Anna' of the sheet is then a later wife, and the wedding date of October 1881 beside her is not explained.
